## PAL5 draft — review, personalize, and post by Tuesday October 6

This week I used Codex assistance to audit the phonebook experiments and
their evaluation. The audit exposed an invalid comparison of L2-SP token
accuracy with replay exact recall. At the original seed42 settings, both
retained all ten A entries but recalled zero complete B numbers. Replay
nevertheless had higher B token accuracy at those cited settings. I also
corrected the inference token budget, required newline-terminated exact
matches, tracked the source datasets, and added data-integrity checks.

A bounded follow-up showed that balanced replay can learn both books:
lr=0.001 with 100 updates produced 10/10 exact A and 10/10 exact B recall for
each of the three available Phase-A checkpoints. With lr=0.0001/100 updates,
B recall was 0/10, 1/10, and 0/10. Seed42 also learned both books with 500
updates at the smaller learning rate, and a fresh A+B control achieved
perfect joint recall at lr=0.001/100 updates. The follow-up checkpoint
conditions were selected after the seed42 pilot, then fixed before running.

All eight training runs finished successfully. An independent audit checked
400 raw completions, summaries, manifests, finite/complete training CSVs,
and artifact hashes. The final full test suite passed all 102 tests without
skips or warnings. These observations concern one fixed 20-entry dataset and
Phase-B seed42; they do not show general replay superiority or the minimum
number of updates needed. My next discussion question is which extension
would best test robustness: larger tasks, Phase-B seeds, or a fair L2-SP
comparison at a successful B-learning budget.
