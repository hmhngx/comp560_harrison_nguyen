# Defense sheet: how many entries a tiny transformer can memorize (E4 to E7)

*Status as of November 14, 2026. A study sheet for explaining the capacity experiments without help: E4 and E6 (width, rungs and training length, with the weight decay and the learning rate held fixed) and E7 (which then varied the weight decay and the learning-rate schedule). Every number in it is rechecked against the committed results by `tests/test_defense_sheets.py`, and the numbers table says where each one comes from. Companion sheet: [replay ratio (E3 to E5)](replay-ratio-e3-e5.md). All claims and their evidence: [RESULTS.md](../../RESULTS.md).*

## 1. The question

How many random name-to-number entries can a very small transformer memorize exactly, and what limits it: the size of the model or the time it is trained? The course README asks for the capacity of a very small model, found by training on larger and larger phonebooks until the model can no longer recall every entry. E4 measured the capacity at four training budgets for four widths; E6 trained the first rung that failed for longer, 4000 epochs instead of 1000 [C02]. E6 left width 64 open: its 640-entry rung did not pass in 4000 epochs [C17, C20]. It also held two settings fixed that could be the limit, the weight decay and the learning rate. E7 changed one of them at a time on the same books: the weight decay set to 0.0 instead of 0.1 (arm W), and the learning rate multiplied by 0.1 after epoch 2000 (arm S) [C30].

## 3. The design

- **What varied.** The width of the model (embedding width 8, 16, 32 and 64, always with 2 layers and 2 heads [C02]); the size of the phonebook, a ladder of rungs for each width [C03]; the training budget, read at 100, 300, 500 and 1000 epochs from one 1000-epoch log per run in E4 [C04] and extended to 4000 epochs for one rung per width in E6 [C02]; and, in E7, one optimizer setting per arm, on the first rung that failed in E4 at widths 16, 32 and 64: the weight decay (0.0 in arm W) or the learning rate (multiplied by 0.1 after epoch 2000 in arm S) [C30, C31].
- **What was held fixed.** In E4 and E6: learning rate 0.001, batch size 64, AdamW with weight decay 0.1 and gradient clip 1.0, and no learning-rate schedule [C02, C28], so an E4 or E6 capacity is a capacity at those settings. E7 kept E6's books, seeds, model, base learning rate, batch size and epochs and changed one setting per arm [C30]; arm S recalls the same number of entries as E6 at every logged epoch up to 2000 [C43].
- **The unit of replication.** The book seed: three book seeds per rung with the training seed equal to the book seed, 96 runs in E4 [C03] and 18 in E7 [C31], and a rung is judged by at least 2 of its 3 seeds [C29, C44]. Books are nested: the book of N entries is the first N lines of one large random book per seed.
- **The rules, fixed before the runs.** A run is perfect by a budget if every entry is recalled exactly at some logged epoch up to it (logged every 10 epochs [C02]); a rung passes if at least 2 of its 3 book seeds are perfect; the capacity is the largest rung such that it and every smaller rung pass [C29]. Consecutive rungs differ by a factor of 1.40 to 2.00, so a capacity is known only to the nearest rung [C27]. E7 used the same pass rule and added one for holding: a run holds the book if at least 50 of its 100 logged epochs in 3001 to 4000 have every entry recalled [C44].
- **The measure.** Exact recall. A run counts as perfect from the first logged epoch at which every entry is recalled (first_perfect), even if the model falls back afterwards, and at widths 16 and 32 in E6 it does, because the 100% appears only on isolated logged epochs [C19]. E7 reads two more things from the same logs: whether a run is at full recall at the last logged epoch, epoch 4000 [C34], and whether it holds [C36].
- **Controls and checks.** A control book of 20 entries that share no name or number with training must stay at exact recall 0 [C13]; in E7 it stayed at 0 of 20 in all 18 runs [C42]. The parameter count of every run must equal the closed form for its model, and E6 must reproduce the first 1000 epochs of E4 [C24]. The runs were audited, and the pre-registered predictions were scored mechanically [C25]; the E7 predictions too, with 41 of 44 scored lines hit [C40].

## 5. The mechanism

**Time, not room.** The reading of E4 and E6 is that the limit they found is mostly training time rather than room in the weights. E7 sharpened it at width 64: with the weight decay at 0.0 the widest model passes its 640-entry rung [C30, C31, C32], so the decay was an obstacle there, which RESULTS.md calls Supported, and it is not shown to be the only one. It is a reading with limits, stated here; at weight decay 0.1 width 64 is not shown to pass [C20, C28].

- The first epoch at which the 10-entry book is fully recalled falls as the width grows: 470 to 790, 200 to 220, 110 to 120 and 60 to 70 epochs for widths 8, 16, 32 and 64 [C11].
- The boundary moves with the budget at three of the four widths, so those capacities are budget limits; it is stable only at width 32 [C10].
- E6 supports the reading where it could: at 4000 epochs the first rung that failed at 1000 epochs passes at widths 8, 16 and 32, so their capacities are at least 20, 160 and 320 entries [C15, C17].
- At weight decay 0.1 [C28], width 64 is not shown to pass: its 640-entry rung did not pass in 4000 epochs in E6, the best recall at any logged epoch was 637, 638 and 636 of 640 [C20], and the pooled mean accuracy rose from 0.9684 (epochs 1001 to 2000) to 0.9754 (epochs 3001 to 4000), a gain of +0.0070 [C21], which the pre-registered threshold calls rising [C22]. A slow rise is no evidence that 100% will be reached or that it will not [C19]. Width 64 at 640 entries is not shown to be possible and not shown to be impossible at that decay [C20, C28].
- In E6, at widths 16 and 32 the 100% is reached only on isolated logged epochs (3, 21, 2 and 6, 1, 0 of 400 per seed [C19]), and at epoch 4000 the runs recall 147 to 158 of 160 and 311 to 316 of 320 [C18]; so "passes" there means a visit to full recall, not a held state.
- In E4 the evidence that the rung above each capacity needs more epochs was strong only for widths 8 and 16; at widths 32 and 64 accuracy had flattened just short of full recall (the E4 results in the [week README](../../experiments/week_2026-10-31/README.md)) [C04, C05, C06, C07].
- E7 changed one setting per arm on the same books and seeds as E6 [C30, C31]. With the weight decay at 0.0 (arm W) every rung passes, including width 64's 640 entries, by 2000 epochs at every width; with the learning rate multiplied by 0.1 after epoch 2000 (arm S) every rung passes by 3000 [C30, C31, C32].
- Arm W changes only the weight decay, from 0.1 to 0.0, and the wide models then hold their full recall where E6's did not: at widths 32 and 64 in every seed, and at width 16 in two of its three seeds [C30, C35, C36]. That is the evidence behind the status Supported; it does not show that the decay is the only factor, that 0.1 is special, or anything at other shapes, learning rates or data, and each width has three book seeds [C30, C31].
- Arm S is the same run as E6 up to epoch 2000 (the same number of entries recalled at every logged epoch in 9 of 9 runs [C43]), so everything it changes happens after the step-down: 6 of its 9 runs first reach full recall at epoch 2010, the first logged epoch after it [C33], and all 9 hold [C36]. AdamW decays every weight by the learning rate times the weight decay at each step, so the step-down lowers the step size and the per-step decay together, and a step-down that keeps the per-step decay was not run. That the lower step size, rather than the lower decay, is what lets the models hold is Not established.
- In E6 none of the nine runs at widths 16, 32 and 64 held full recall; in E7 arm S holds in 9 of 9 runs and arm W in 8 of 9 [C36]. The exception is arm W at width 16 with seed 1: 13 of its last 100 logged epochs at full recall, isolated as in E6, although it ends at full recall [C34, C35]. Two arm W runs at width 64 settle and then dip, to 390 and to 25 of 640, and are back at full recall at epoch 4000 [C37].
- What this does not separate: E4 and E6 changed the width and the training length only, never the learning rate, the schedule or the weight decay [C28], and E7 changed the decay and the schedule only to 0.0 and to one step-down, on the same books, so other decays, a different constant learning rate, other shapes and other data could change the picture [C30].

## 6. What was not shown

- Whether any rung is out of reach for any width. No width has been shown to have an impossible rung, and a run of finite length cannot show it; what E4, E6 and E7 show is that a rung was or was not reached within a budget, under the settings in [C28] and [C30].
- An asymptotic capacity: every capacity here is a lower bound. E6 lifted the E4 capacities at three widths [C17], E7 lifted the fourth, at width 64, in both arms [C32], and E7 ran one rung per width, so the rung above each pass was not run [C31].
- That the models always hold the full recall they reach. In E6 they did not at widths 16 and 32, where the 100% is isolated and none of the six runs ends at it [C18, C19]. In E7 arm S holds in 9 of 9 runs and arm W in 8 of 9 [C36]; arm W at width 16 with seed 1 does not (13 of its last 100 logged epochs at full recall [C35]), and two arm W runs at width 64 dip after settling [C37].
- That the weight decay is the only factor, or that 0.1 is special: arm W changed it from 0.1 to 0.0 and nothing else, with three book seeds per width [C30, C31]; other values, other learning rates, other shapes and other data were not tried.
- That the lower step size, rather than the lower weight decay, is what helps in arm S: AdamW decays every weight by the learning rate times the weight decay, so the step-down changes both, and a step-down that keeps the per-step decay was not run.
- The effect of a different constant learning rate: the base learning rate was 0.001 in every run, and arm S only lowered it after epoch 2000 [C30].
- The effect of depth or of the number of heads: only the width was reduced, with 2 layers and 2 heads throughout [C02].
- Anything beyond memorization of the training pairs: the control book was never recalled, 0 of 20 in every run of E4, E6 and E7 [C13, C42], so this says nothing about generalization to new entries.
- Anything about other data than random name-to-number entries.

## 7. Sixteen hard questions

**Q1. What exactly is "capacity" here, and why is it a lower bound?**
Answer. The capacity at a budget is the largest rung of the width's ladder such that it and every smaller rung pass, and a rung passes when at least 2 of its 3 book seeds recalled every entry exactly at some logged epoch up to the budget [C29]. It is a lower bound because the rung above it was only observed not to pass within that budget, because rungs are spaced by a factor of 1.40 to 2.00 [C27], and because a longer run can pass more: E6 lifted three of the four capacities [C17], and E7 lifted the fourth, at width 64, with the weight decay at 0.0 or a step-down [C30, C32].
Must mention. the largest passing rung with all smaller rungs passing; at least 2 of 3 seeds; recalled at some logged epoch up to the budget; the spacing of the rungs; E6 lifted three capacities and E7 the fourth [C17, C29, C32].

**Q2. What are the capacities, and how do they scale with width?**
Answer. At 1000 epochs they are 14, 113, 226 and 453 entries at widths 8, 16, 32 and 64 [C04, C05, C06, C07], with 3,064 / 9,216 / 30,720 / 110,656 parameters [C08], which is 0.149 / 0.399 / 0.240 / 0.133 bits per parameter [C09], far below one. From width 16 to 32 and from 32 to 64 the capacity doubles (ratios 2.00 and 2.00) while the parameter count grows by 3.33 and 3.60 [C12]. A capacity is known only to the nearest rung, and neighbouring rungs differ by a factor of 1.40 to 2.00 [C27]. After E7, in either arm, the largest rungs known to pass at widths 16, 32 and 64 are 160, 320 and 640 entries, at least 0.566, 0.339 and 0.188 bits per parameter [C41].
Must mention. 14, 113, 226 and 453 at widths 8, 16, 32 and 64; bits per parameter 0.133 to 0.399, far below one; capacity doubles when the width doubles while parameters grow faster; the resolution is one rung; after E7 at least 160, 320 and 640 entries at widths 16, 32 and 64 [C04, C05, C06, C07, C09, C12, C41].

**Q3. Why do you say the limit is training time, not room in the weights?**
Answer. Three observations from E4 and E6. The first epoch at which the 10-entry book is fully recalled falls with width: 470 to 790, 200 to 220, 110 to 120 and 60 to 70 epochs for widths 8, 16, 32 and 64 [C11]. The boundary moves with the budget at three widths and is stable only at width 32 [C10]. And at 4000 epochs the first rung that failed at 1000 epochs passes at widths 8, 16 and 32 [C17]. All of this is at one learning rate, weight decay and schedule [C28], so in E4 and E6 "time" is shorthand for epochs at those settings, and E6 does not separate longer training from the learning rate, the schedule or the weight decay. E7 then varied the decay: with it at 0.0 the 640-entry rung at width 64 passes by 2000 epochs [C30, C31, C32], so at width 64 what stopped E6 was the weight decay, not a lack of room in the weights, which RESULTS.md marks Supported; that the decay is the only factor is not shown.
Must mention. the first full recall of the smallest book comes sooner with width; the boundary moves with the budget at three widths; E6 passes at widths 8, 16 and 32; one learning rate, weight decay and schedule in E4 and E6; with the decay at 0.0 width 64 passes (Supported, not shown to be the only factor) [C10, C11, C17, C28, C30, C32].

**Q4. What did E6 change, and what did it leave open?**
Answer. E6 trained the first rung that failed at 1000 epochs for 4000 epochs: it passes at widths 8, 16 and 32 (first passing budgets 2000, 4000 and 3000) and not at width 64 [C17]. So E4's capacities of 14, 113 and 226 entries at those widths were budget limits [C04, C05, C06], and the capacities are now at least 20, 160 and 320 entries [C15], at least 0.213, 0.566 and 0.339 bits per parameter, still under one bit [C23]. It left open width 64, the rungs above E6's passes (not run), and everything about the learning rate, schedule and weight decay [C17, C28]; E7 took up width 64 and the weight decay and the schedule (Q13), and the rungs above the passes are still not run [C31].
Must mention. the first failing rung trained for 4000 epochs; passes at widths 8, 16 and 32 but not 64; E4's capacities were budget limits; lower bounds; E6 left width 64 and the unvaried settings open, which E7 took up [C17, C23].

**Q5. Some passes rest on isolated epochs. Is that really a pass?**
Answer. By the rule, yes: a rung passes when every entry was recalled at some logged epoch, and that happened, in 3, 21, 2 and 6, 1, 0 of 400 logged epochs per seed at widths 16 and 32 [C19]. But the models do not hold it: at epoch 4000 they recall 147 to 158 of 160 and 311 to 316 of 320 [C18], none of the six runs ends at full recall, and width 32 seed 3 never reached it (closest: 319 of 320 at logged epoch 740 [C26]). The first epochs at full recall are 3270, 1210 and 3200 at width 16 and 940, 2960 and never at width 32 [C16]. So the passes are visits, not stable states, which is why the claims table marks the claim that a wide model keeps its 100% as not true in E6 [C19]. E7 is the contrast: 8 of 9 arm W runs and 9 of 9 arm S runs hold their full recall, and the arm W run that does not (width 16, seed 1) is isolated in the same way, 13 of its last 100 logged epochs [C35, C36].
Must mention. the rule counts the first epoch at full recall; 3, 21, 2 and 6, 1, 0 of 400 logged epochs; the recall at epoch 4000 is below full; a pass is a visit, not a held state; in E7 most runs do hold [C18, C19, C36].

**Q6. Is the widest model's 640-entry rung out of reach [C20], and what is the evidence either way?**
Answer. At weight decay 0.1 it was not reached in E6: no seed reached 640 of 640 in 4000 epochs, the best recall at any logged epoch was 637, 638 and 636 [C20], and at epoch 4000 it was 610, 633 and 623 [C18]; the pooled mean accuracy rose from 0.9684 to 0.9754, a gain of +0.0070, which the pre-registered threshold calls rising [C21, C22], and a slow rise is no evidence that 100% will be reached or that it will not [C19, C28]. E7 answers the question for its settings: with the weight decay at 0.0 (arm W) all three seeds reach 640 of 640 by epoch 1500 [C30, C31, C33], the rung passes by 2000 epochs [C32] and all three end at full recall [C34]; with the learning rate cut after epoch 2000 (arm S) all three first reach it at epoch 2010 [C33] and the rung passes by 3000 [C32]. So the rung is not out of reach, and E7 supports the reading that the weight decay of 0.1 was what stopped E6 [C28]. A finite run still cannot show a rung impossible, and nothing above 640 entries was run [C31].
Must mention. at weight decay 0.1 no seed reached 640 of 640 (best 637, 638 and 636) and the slow rise settled nothing; in E7 the rung passes in both arms (arm W by 2000 epochs, arm S by 3000); so it is not out of reach, the decay is supported as the obstacle but not shown to be the only one; a finite run cannot show impossibility and nothing above 640 was run [C20, C28, C31, C32].

**Q7. What do the prediction hit counts show?**
Answer. E4: 23 of 23 scored lines hit (13 exact); E6: 17 of 17 (8 exact) [C25]; E7: 41 of 44 (25 exact) [C40]. That is less than it sounds: the lines are strongly correlated (the same four capacities read at different budgets; the seeds-perfect, pass and first-budget lines of E6 all read the same 12 runs [C13], and the 24 E7 lines on seeds perfect, passing, first passing budget and ending at 100% all read the same 18 runs and all hit [C31, C40]), and the calibration behind the predictions used a different book seed and different training seeds, which is why they generalized. One part of the locked E4 explanation was only half right: the rung above each boundary was said to be still climbing at the end, which holds for widths 8 and 16 and not for widths 32 and 64 [C04, C05, C06, C07]. The three E7 misses are all about arm W: the number of holding seeds at width 16, the arm W mean accuracy over epochs 3001 to 4000 at width 64, and which arm holds more seeds at width 16 [C40]. The E7 predictions were drafted by Claude at the researcher's request, settled with the researcher and adopted by the researcher before any run, so the count also says how well that drafting, and the researcher's choices among its options, read the E6 logs.
Must mention. 23 of 23, 17 of 17 and 41 of 44 scored lines hit; the lines are correlated; the calibration used other seeds; one E4 explanation was half right; the three E7 misses are about arm W; the E7 predictions were drafted by Claude and adopted by the researcher before any run [C25, C40].

**Q8. Why only three book seeds, and what does a majority rule cost?**
Answer. There are three seeds per rung, 96 runs in E4 [C03], and the rule is a majority, at least 2 of 3 [C29]. A majority is coarse: a rung whose third seed fails still passes, and several capacities rest on cells at the edge of a budget. Width 8 passes at 14 entries only because one seed finished at epoch 1000, the last logged epoch, while another never did (first full recall at epochs 1000, none and 730 for seeds 1, 2, 3 [C14]). So the claims are stated for this budget, rule and criterion, and as lower bounds. E7 repeats the design, three seeds per rung in each arm and 18 runs [C31]; with three seeds a single run changes the count of holding seeds at a width, as at width 16 in arm W, where seed 1 does not hold and the other two do [C35]. That is why the claim about the weight decay is Supported and not Established.
Must mention. three seeds and a 2-of-3 majority; a rung can pass with a failing seed; width 8 at 14 entries rests on a seed that finished at the last logged epoch; the claims are lower bounds; E7 has the same three seeds, so its decay claim is Supported, not Established [C03, C14, C29, C31].

**Q9. Why bits per parameter, and what does the value at width 16 mean [C09]?**
Answer. An entry's phone number is one of 800 x 800 x 10,000 equally likely values, so about 32.575 bits [C01]. Bits per parameter is the entries times 32.575 divided by the parameters [C01]: 0.149 / 0.399 / 0.240 / 0.133 at 1000 epochs [C09], and at least 0.213 / 0.566 / 0.339 / 0.133 after E6 [C23]; after E7 at least 0.566 / 0.339 / 0.188 at widths 16, 32 and 64 in both arms, so width 64's value is above its 0.133 at 1000 epochs [C41, C09]. It is a yardstick, not a measured storage: the name is the question and is not counted, and the number says how far below one bit per parameter these models stayed, not that they could not go higher.
Must mention. 32.575 bits per entry from 800 x 800 x 10,000 numbers; entries times bits per entry over parameters; 0.399 at width 16; far below one; a yardstick, not a measured storage; E7 at least 0.188 at width 64 [C01, C09, C41].

**Q10. What was checked to make sure the control and the pipeline were not fooling you?**
Answer. The control book (20 entries never trained on, sharing no name or number with training) was never recalled: 0 of 20 in all 96 E4 runs and all 12 E6 runs [C13], so the recall is memorization of the training pairs. The first 1000 epochs of every E6 run equal the E4 run [C24], so E6 continues E4's runs instead of drawing new ones. The runs were audited, and the analysis was recomputed with separate code. In E7 the control book was never recalled either, 0 of 20 in all 18 runs [C42], and arm S recalls the same number of entries as E6 at every logged epoch up to 2000 in 9 of 9 runs [C43], so the step-down at epoch 2000 is the first place it can differ from E6 [C30].
Must mention. 0 of 20 control entries in all 96 and 12 runs; the first 1000 epochs of E6 equal E4's for 12 of 12 runs; audits and a separate recomputation; E7: 0 of 20 in all 18 runs and arm S equal to E6 up to epoch 2000 [C13, C24, C42, C43].

**Q11. How would you find out whether the widest model is just slow, or held back by an optimizer setting?**
Answer. E7 did that for two settings: it trained the 640-entry rung for 4000 epochs with the weight decay at 0.0 (arm W) and with the learning rate cut after epoch 2000 (arm S), and the rung passes in both [C30, C31, C32]. What is left: a longer run at weight decay 0.1 [C28]; a step-down that keeps the per-step decay, which would separate the step size from the decay in arm S; other values of the decay and of the learning rate; and the rungs above 640 entries, which were not run [C31]. Fix the pass rule and write the predictions before any run, as E4, E6 and E7 did. The best possible answer is still "reached" or "not reached within the budget under these settings"; a finite run cannot show a rung impossible.
Must mention. E7 already ran the weight decay at 0.0 and a step-down; a longer run at weight decay 0.1; a step-down that keeps the per-step decay; other decays and learning rates; the answer is reached or not reached within a budget [C28, C30, C31, C32].

**Q12. What is the one-sentence takeaway you would defend, and what would you refuse to claim?**
Answer. A 2-layer, 2-head transformer memorizes at least 20, 160 and 320 random name-to-number entries at widths 8, 16 and 32 after 4000 epochs, and at least 453 at width 64 after 1000 epochs [C15, C17, C07], and at least 640 at width 64 in E7's arms [C41], all far below one bit per parameter [C23, C41]; the limit E4 and E6 found is mostly training time at their settings [C02], with the weight decay of 0.1 a named obstacle at width 64, which RESULTS.md marks Supported [C28, C30]. I would refuse to claim an asymptotic capacity, an impossible rung, that the decay is the only factor or that 0.1 is special, that the lower step size rather than the lower decay is what helps in arm S, that these models always hold full recall once they reach it [C19, C36], or anything about other learning rates, decays, depths or head counts [C28, C30].
Must mention. at least 20, 160, 320 and 453 entries, and 640 at width 64 in E7; far below one bit per parameter; mostly training time at E4's and E6's settings, the decay a named obstacle at width 64 (Supported); no asymptotic capacity, no impossible rung, no claim that the decay is the only factor, none about unvaried settings [C07, C15, C23, C28, C41].

**Q13. What did E7 change, and what did it find?**
Answer. E7 retrained the three rungs that E6 trained for 4000 epochs, at widths 16, 32 and 64 (160, 320 and 640 entries), on the same books and seeds, changing one optimizer setting per arm: the weight decay at 0.0 instead of 0.1 (arm W), or the learning rate multiplied by 0.1 after epoch 2000 (arm S) [C30, C31]. Every rung passes in both arms, including width 64, which E6 never passed: arm W by 2000 epochs at every width and arm S by 3000 [C32]. All 18 runs are at full recall at epoch 4000, against none of E6's nine at these widths [C31, C34]. RESULTS.md calls this Established, by the pass rule fixed before the runs [C44].
Must mention. one setting per arm on E6's books and seeds; every rung passes, including width 64; arm W by 2000 epochs and arm S by 3000; 18 of 18 at full recall at epoch 4000 against 0 of 9 in E6; Established by the pre-registered rule [C30, C31, C32, C34, C44].

**Q14. Does E7 show that the weight decay was what stopped E6, and how sure are you?**
Answer. It supports it, and Supported is the word RESULTS.md uses. Arm W changes only the weight decay, from 0.1 to 0.0, on E6's books and seeds [C30]. Then width 64's 640-entry rung passes by 2000 epochs [C31, C32], the wide models hold their full recall at widths 32 and 64 in every seed and at width 16 in two of three seeds, where E6's did not [C35, C36], and two tripwires that the predictions file had tied to this reading fired [C40]. What is not shown: that the decay is the only factor, that 0.1 is special (only 0.1 and 0.0 were run [C30]), or anything at other shapes, learning rates or data; and each width has three book seeds [C31].
Must mention. only the weight decay changes in arm W; the 640-entry rung passes and the models hold where E6's did not; Supported, not Established; not shown that the decay is the only factor or that 0.1 is special; three book seeds [C30, C31, C32, C35, C36, C40].

**Q15. What does arm S show, and why can you not say that the lower step size is what helps?**
Answer. Arm S multiplies the learning rate by 0.1 after epoch 2000 and keeps the weight decay at 0.1 [C30]. It is the same run as E6 until then [C43]; after the step-down every rung passes (first at epoch 2010 in 6 of its 9 runs [C33]) and all 9 runs hold their full recall [C36]. But AdamW decays every weight by the learning rate times the weight decay at each step, so the step-down lowers the step size and the per-step decay together, and arm S cannot say which of the two does the work; only arm W isolates the decay. A step-down that keeps the per-step decay was not run. RESULTS.md therefore marks the claim that the lower step size is what lets the models hold as Not established.
Must mention. arm S cuts the learning rate after epoch 2000 and keeps the weight decay; the same run as E6 until then; every rung passes and all nine runs hold; AdamW's decay is the learning rate times the weight decay, so both fall; only arm W isolates the decay; Not established [C30, C33, C36, C43].

**Q16. Do the E7 models hold their full recall, and what are the exceptions?**
Answer. By the rule fixed before the runs, a run holds if at least 50 of its last 100 logged epochs (3001 to 4000) are at full recall [C44]. Arm S holds in 9 of 9 runs, arm W in 8 of 9, and E6 in 0 of 9 at these widths [C36]. The exception is arm W at width 16 with seed 1: 13 of the last 100 logged epochs at full recall, isolated as in E6, although it ends at full recall [C34, C35]. Two arm W runs at width 64 settle and then dip, to 390 and to 25 of 640, and are back at full recall at epoch 4000; the second dip lasts until logged epoch 3990, so that run's full recall at epoch 4000 rests on a recovery in the last logged interval [C37]. The two dips hold the arm W mean accuracy at width 64 over 3001 to 4000 at 0.9928, and its trend is flat; that mean is one of the three misses [C38, C39, C40].
Must mention. the holding rule (at least 50 of the last 100 logged epochs); arm S 9 of 9, arm W 8 of 9, E6 0 of 9; the exception arm W at width 16 with seed 1 (13 of 100); two width-64 dips to 390 and 25 of 640, back at full recall by epoch 4000 [C35, C36, C37, C44].

## 8. Glossary

- **exact recall**: an entry counts as recalled only if greedy generation from its name produces the whole phone number and the newline, with no length hint.
- **entry**: one name-to-phone-number pair; its number carries about 32.575 bits [C01].
- **epoch**: one pass over the training book; the batch size is 64, so a book larger than one batch takes several updates per epoch [C02].
- **first_perfect**: the first logged epoch at which every entry of the book is recalled exactly (logged every 10 epochs [C02]); none if it never happens.
- **budget**: the number of epochs a run is allowed; E4 reads 100, 300, 500 and 1000 epochs from one 1000-epoch log per run [C04], E6 and E7 read 1000, 2000, 3000 and 4000 [C17, C32].
- **rung**: one book size N on a width's ladder; books are nested, so a larger rung contains the smaller ones [C03].
- **capacity**: at a budget, the largest rung such that it and every smaller rung pass; a rung passes when at least 2 of its 3 book seeds are perfect by the budget [C29].
- **lower bound**: a capacity that is at least this much, because the rung above was not run or did not pass within the budget [C17].
- **boundary**: "stable" if the capacity at 500 epochs equals the capacity at 1000, otherwise "moving", which means the number is a budget limit and not a capacity [C10].
- **bits per parameter**: entries times the bits per entry, divided by the parameters; a yardstick of how full the model is [C09].
- **arm**: one of the two variants of E7, each changing one optimizer setting from E6: arm W sets the weight decay to 0.0, arm S multiplies the learning rate by 0.1 after epoch 2000 [C30].
- **weight decay**: the AdamW setting that shrinks every weight by the learning rate times the weight decay at each step; 0.1 in E4 and E6 and in arm S, 0.0 in arm W [C28, C30].
- **step-down**: a cut of the learning rate to a tenth of its value after a given epoch, here after epoch 2000 in arm S; with AdamW it lowers the step size and the per-step decay together [C30].
- **holds**: a run holds the book if at least 50 of its last 100 logged epochs (3001 to 4000) have every entry recalled [C44].
- **dip**: after a run has settled (50 consecutive logged epochs at full recall), a logged epoch below full recall; the pre-registration asked for the dips of arm W runs to be listed [C37].
